# Audit: the scanner ranks on win rate and loses money doing it

**Date:** 2026-11-25 · **Scope:** StrategyScanner pick selection, GEX Walls track record
**Data:** `data/picks.csv.gz`, 9,456 logged picks / 3,478 settled, 2026-10-19 to 2026-11-25
**Reproduce:** `python analysis/audit_picks.py data/picks.csv.gz`

This is a findings document for the other agents working on this repo (see `AGENTS.md`). Every number
below is computed from the pick database shipped alongside it. Where the data cannot support a
conclusion, that is stated rather than papered over.

---

## 1. Headline

Over 3,478 settled picks the scanner's trades won **71% of the time** and **lost money**: an average
of **−10.1% of capital at risk per trade**, −$97,193 in total at one contract per pick.

A 71% win rate and a loss are not a contradiction. The average win was **+$47** and the average loss
**−$211**. At those payoffs the break-even win rate is 82%.

The predicted win rate was 78.2%, so the probability model was also overconfident by 7.3 points —
but that is the smaller problem. **Even if its probabilities had been perfect, the trades it picked
would still have lost money**, because nothing in the selection logic ever compared what a trade wins
against what it loses.

## 2. The defect, precisely

`config.json` gates on `min_pop`, `min_roc`, `min_profit`, `min_premium_roc`, `min_roc_annual`,
`max_capital` and liquidity. `ranker.sort_key` ordered candidates by probability of profit, then
lowest capital, then return.

There was **no expected-value term anywhere** — in the filters or the ranking.

Expected value of each logged pick at the scanner's *own* probability, two-outcome
(win → keep the credit, lose → pay max loss):

| assumption | mean EV per trade | share with EV ≤ 0 |
|---|---|---|
| mid fills, no commission | −$11.52 | 55.7% |
| +$0.01/leg slippage, $0.65/leg commission | −$17.28 | 73.0% |
| +$0.03/leg slippage, $0.65/leg commission | −$22.29 | 80.6% |

Probability of profit says how *often* a trade wins. It is silent on how much. Ranking by it selects
trades that win often and lose more than they win — which is what the realised results show.

Sorting the settled picks into quintiles by predicted EV confirms EV carries signal the old ranking
threw away:

| EV quintile | predicted EV | realised avg | win rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (worst) | −$137.28 | **−$74.82** | 64% |
| 2 | −$10.18 | −$51.63 | 59% |
| 3 | −$2.80 | −$17.88 | 66% |
| 4 | +$1.65 | **+$8.73** | 86% |
| 5 (best) | +$20.65 | −$4.22 | 79% |

Monotone from 1 to 4, then breaking at quintile 5 — the highest-EV candidates are the widest and
riskiest, so EV alone is not a sufficient objective either. Expected value **per dollar at risk** is
the better one, and is what the fix ranks on.

## 3. Why the losses arrived all at once

Per-expiry results, which is the real unit of risk:

```
expiry       trades  win%  pred%      P/L $    capital at risk
2026-11-06      342   75%   77%    -11,070    $    94,768
2026-11-13      686   81%   75%     +8,524    $   163,306
2026-11-18      107   97%   81%     +5,395    $    27,303
2026-11-20     1217   53%   78%    -99,879    $   367,900   <-- the whole loss
2026-11-23      144   87%   75%     +4,659    $    45,392
```

**25 of 26 expiries together: +$2,686. The 26th: −$99,879.**

On 2026-11-20 the record held **1,217 positions and $367,900 of capital at risk on a single
expiration date** — 29× the median expiry's exposure — and **68% of that risk pointed the same
direction** (short puts and the put wings of condors). A broad selloff into that date took every
one of them at once: TSM 412→319, MU 729→595, BE 221→142, UNH 399→340.

That is not 1,217 independent bets. It is one bet, sized 1,217 times. The call-spread side won 99%
of its trades the same week, which is the proof that one market move decided everything.

**Statistical consequence, and it matters for every claim made from this data:** the 3,478 trades sit
on only **560 ticker/expiry clusters** and 26 expiries. Outcomes inside a cluster move together, so
naive error bars are **2.4× too tight**. Clustered on ticker/expiry, the average result per trade is
−10.1% **±4.2 points** and the dollar figure is ±$1,300. Any A/B comparison on this dataset needs
≥20 independent clusters per arm before it means anything (`ARENA.md` already says this).

## 4. The deeper cause: the volatility forecast

Short premium only pays when implied volatility exceeds the movement that actually turns up. So:
how did the forecast do?

For each independent ticker/expiry, the realised move in units of the forecast's own sigma
(499 observations):

| forecast | RMS of realised/forecast | needed stress |
|---|---|---|
| `rv20` (what the scanner used) | **1.712** | 1.71× |
| `iv30` (the market's forecast) | 1.561 | 1.56× |
| `max(rv20, iv30)` | 1.491 | 1.49× |
| `max(rv20, iv30) × 1.25` | **1.193** | 1.19× |

Stocks moved **1.71× further than the scanner's volatility forecast implied**, while options were
being sold at a median `iv30/rv20` of only **1.08**. Only **3%** of picks had enough implied premium
to cover the movement that actually happened. Mean move was **−0.86 sigma** — a persistent downward
drift no symmetric model captures.

**Is `rv20` simply broken?** No — and this is the check that stops the fix being overfitted to five
bad weeks. Across **110,318** forward/trailing 20-day volatility pairs over two years of cached
history (1,288 tickers):

```
forward 20d vol / trailing 20d vol:   median 0.998   mean 1.121   RMS 1.362
                                      90th pct 1.738   share above 1.0: 50%
by month: 2026-10 median 1.42  <- worst month in two years
          typical month 0.92 - 1.13
```

`rv20` is **median-unbiased** over two years. But the distribution is **right-skewed** — volatility
spikes further than it falls — and **expected value is an average, not a median**. A median-unbiased
volatility forecast therefore systematically understates what short premium expects to pay. The
honest multiplier is the mean-to-RMS range, **1.12 to 1.36**, and Oct–Nov 2026 ran at 1.42.

**Regime scoreboard** (realised move ÷ what implied volatility was paying for, median per expiry;
above 1.00 means selling premium lost):

```
10-23 0.89 | 10-26 0.63 | 10-30 0.73 | 11-04 0.95 | 11-06 1.51 | 11-09 0.58
11-11 0.69 | 11-13 0.87 | 11-16 0.73 | 11-18 1.16 | 11-20 1.65 | 11-23 0.67
WHOLE PERIOD median 1.00
```

Median exactly 1.00 — options were **fairly priced**, not rich. There was no volatility risk premium
to harvest over this period. Selling fairly-priced options with a fat-tailed payoff has negative
expected value, and it shows up exactly as observed: small gains most weeks, two expiries that take
it all back.

## 5. What was changed

Code: `scanner/ev.py` (new), `scanner/strategies.py`, `scanner/ranker.py`, `config.json`.

1. **`scanner/ev.py`** — expected value in closed form, from the same lognormal the POP figures use.
   `E[max(K−S,0)]` and `E[max(S−K,0)]` undiscounted, so the EV of any position built from European
   legs held to expiry is exactly `net credit − E[net intrinsic owed]`. Verified against put–call
   parity (exact to 1e-9), against Monte Carlo (400k paths, agreement to MC error), and by the
   identity that a fairly-priced spread valued at its own volatility must return **EV = 0.00000000**.
2. **Zero drift.** POP uses drift `r`; EV uses `F = S`. With drift `r` a two-year LEAPS call scores
   +8% of spot before it does anything, and **every** long-LEAPS pick passed the gate on that
   artifact alone. Not assuming the market rises is the conservative choice for a filter.
3. **The gate prices expected cost at `rv × rv_stress`, deliberately not at implied.** EV at implied
   volatility is zero by no-arbitrage, so stressing implied rejects every short-premium trade ever
   written instead of discriminating between them. Stressing the *realised* forecast corrects that
   forecast's measured bias and leaves the real question intact. **This was wrong in my first
   attempt** — `max(rv, iv) × stress` rejected 100% of short premium by construction.
4. **`rv_stress = 1.35`**, from the two-year RMS (1.36) and mean (1.12), not from the five bad weeks.
   Calibration check: predicted EV matches the realised average almost exactly at stress **1.71**
   (predicted −$38.37 vs realised −$37.14) — strong evidence the EV model is right when handed the
   right volatility, and evidence that 1.71 was this regime specifically.
5. **`min_iv_rv = 1.05`** — short premium requires implied above the stock's own movement.
6. **Ranking is now expected return per dollar at risk**, with POP kept as a floor. `ev.enabled:
   false` restores the old behaviour.

Pass rates at `rv_stress 1.35` plus the premium test: 9% of all historical picks survive
(PCS 6%, IC 6%, CCS 12%, CSP 20%, CC 23%, LCALL 24%).

## 6. What is NOT claimed

Read this before quoting any number above as evidence a change works.

- **The EV gate is not shown to make money.** On the settled picks it cuts the total loss from
  −$103,781 to −$36,286, but the per-trade average barely moves (−$37.14 → −$34.82) and the
  clustered CI is ±$2,162. It is justified by **arithmetic** — a trade with negative expected value
  at the model's own probability is a logical defect, not a statistical artifact — and not by this
  backtest. At every stress level tested, the surviving picks still lost money.
- **26 expiries, one of which decides the result, cannot establish or refute an edge.** Nothing here
  should be read as "short premium works" or "short premium does not work".
- **The settlement convention is favourable.** Picks settle at the official close on expiration day,
  so assignment risk, early exercise, pin risk and gap risk between the mark and the close are
  absent. Entry assumes a fill a quarter-spread worse than mid (`slippage: 0.5`) and, before this
  change, no commission at all.
- **Only ~63% of picks reach a settlement**; `learn.py` excludes uncounted ones. Selection effects in
  which picks resolve have not been tested.
- The 2-year volatility study uses the GEX Walls daily cache, which is survivorship-biased toward
  tickers liquid enough to be scanned today.

## 7. Open questions for other agents

Ranked by how much they would change the answer. Evidence over opinion; `ARENA.md` scoring applies.

1. **Is there a volatility edge at all, and where?** The median `iv/rv` was 1.08 and realised came in
   at 1.00× implied. If short premium has no edge on this universe, the right fix is not better
   filtering — it is a different strategy. Test across regimes, not these five weeks.
2. **Concentration control.** Nothing yet caps capital per expiry, per ticker, or per direction. The
   record put $367,900 on one date with 68% one-way. What is the right cap, and should the *track
   record* model a sane portfolio rather than every idea the scanner printed?
3. **Directional drift.** Mean realised move was −0.86 sigma. A symmetric lognormal cannot see that.
   Does conditioning on trend/GEX positioning beat ignoring it, out of sample?
4. **Is `pop_cons = min(pop_iv, pop_rv)` the right conservatism?** It was still 7.3 points
   overconfident, and 24 points overconfident in the 70–75% bucket specifically.
5. **Fill realism.** `slippage: 0.5` (quarter-spread) is a guess. Measure it against Joe's actual
   fills in `trades.json` (private repo) and report the distribution, not a point estimate.
6. **Tail hedging.** If the payoff is "small gains, rare large loss", does a cheap long-wing overlay
   improve the geometric return? Test honestly — most such overlays lose on average.
